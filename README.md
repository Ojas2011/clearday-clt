1. The pitch (read this first)
Problem: People plan outdoor time (a run, an outdoor work shift, kids' practice) by checking one temperature number. Air quality, feels-like heat, UV, rain and wind all matter, and they live in separate apps.
Solution: ClearDay combines them into one 0 to 100 score for every hour at several Charlotte locations, finds the best time window, adapts to who you are (casual, runner, sensitive lungs, outdoor worker), and explains the result in plain language.
Tagline: The best hour to be outside, in one score.
Why this can win first place:
• A real problem you can cite with sources (EPA air quality categories, National Weather Service heat guidance). Find and link them in your write-up.
• Live data, not mock data, so the demo is real.
• A map as the visual hook, plus a score that changes when you switch profiles.
• It is honest: when a data source fails, it says so. It never fakes an answer.
• Technical depth you can explain in 20 seconds: two data sources, a scoring engine, a backend, a typed data contract, and an AI layer that cannot break the app.
• Accessible and polished (dark mode, contrast, keyboard use, font size).
Track fit: Most Technical (data pipeline, scoring logic, backend and frontend contract) or Most Creative (the map and personalization).
Important: this is an informational tool, not medical advice. The score thresholds in this guide are demo thresholds invented for practice. In a real pitch, cite the EPA and NWS sources they are based on and say so.
